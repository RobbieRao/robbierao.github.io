LightSpoken

An interactive lighting and kinetic-installation prototype controlled through a Unity interface.

通过 Unity 界面与 UniOSC 通信控制灯光及蜂巢结构的伸缩运动，将实体制作、灯光编排和机械运动结合为可运行的交互原型。

Year
2022
Practice
Interactive lighting · Physical prototype

Four-layer shell and light apertures

We used four layers, each with its own lighting space, together with lighting for the overall structure. The irregular open form left room for the light to spread between layers. Small cutouts corresponded to point sources; long cutouts followed the LED strips. This let the shell produce localized points and longer bands of light within the same object.

The model used 5 mm acrylic and mirror paper. The first model had rough contours, so we used mirror paper to reshape their appearance and soften the visible light. Cut musical-note shapes were attached near the center, with point sources inside the structure producing halos around them.

Unity light-control panel

We divided the Unity lighting panel into modes and parameters, with UniOSC carrying the controls. Single selected one color, Rainbow changed the lights to rainbow colors, and Breath varied brightness rhythmically. The large touch area adjusted color and brightness in HSB; breathing frequency had its own control. This kept a color choice in place when we changed the pace. We tested the single-color, rainbow, and breathing states on the assembled lights.

Telescopic mechanism and motion controls

The second part of the Unity panel controlled the honeycomb mechanism. An internal motor drove a telescopic rod, moving the modules vertically. The interface provided rise, descend, stop, and reset. Stop retained the current arrangement; reset returned the mechanism to its reference position. Patterns cut into the outer shell extended the visual effect beyond the actuator. As the module moved, its openings and overlap changed the visible pattern around the object. The design paired the vertical travel with the shell pattern so viewers could follow the changing light as well as the mechanism itself.

Module clearance and strip routing

During movement tests, we had to leave gaps between modules to prevent rubbing and collisions. The wiring needed enough freedom for the moving parts as well. We tried one connected light strip to reduce the load, then had to work around cable tangling during assembly. Packing the structure more tightly would have made those problems worse.

We also had to conceal the strip outside the intended apertures. An exposed segment could light up a joint or cable instead of the cut pattern. Clearance, cable routing, and shielding therefore needed checking at different module positions, alongside the lighting controls.

For review, I separate the color control, breathing rhythm, and mechanical position. A fixed light mode makes rubbing, cable tension, and exposed strips easier to see during travel. Holding the mechanism at one position makes the HSB and breathing controls easier to judge. Stop and reset serve different checks: stop holds a troublesome arrangement for inspection; reset returns all modules to their reference positions. Changing every control together can hide whether an uneven patch of light comes from the selected mode, an aperture, or a moving cable.
