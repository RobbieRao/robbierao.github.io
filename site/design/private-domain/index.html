Service design · Community operations

Private Domain UX Design

Designing the campus-service journey from discovery and ordering to support across WeChat channels.

Year
2021–2022
Practice
Service design · Community operations
My role
Project framing, service-model planning, and operating strategy.

A student arrives with a task

In BIZZLE, I worked on how the campus services and their operating model fit together. We used China Academy of Art as the pilot and began with delivery, printing and shopping. A student opening the page for printing needs a short route to that service; someone browsing campus discussion can take more time. Those visits should not be treated as the same kind of traffic.

I put the priorities in the order campus, task, then support. The location choice establishes whether a service applies; direct entries lead to printing, delivery and supplies. Discussion and promotion can have their own places without blocking those tasks. The pilot screens made it possible to inspect that order against actual menu items rather than a broad promise to connect campus life.

Design the way back for help

Students might discover a service through BIZZLE, while another organization carries it out. I separated those responsibilities in the service model: campus announcements, after-sales help, content and community management sit with BIZZLE; the delivery and printing branches connect to their respective operators.

I traced the outward and return routes together. An announcement leads to an entry and a service mini program; a later question needs an equally recognizable path back to instructions or support. Delivery is marked as a commercial partnership, while printing is directly operated. The shared campus brand must not hide who handles the next step.

Do not make the group chat hold everything

The plan used official-account posts, campus WeChat groups, a mini program and a website. I gave them different jobs. An article can explain a service and remain available for reference. The mini program holds task and order navigation. A campus group carries local announcements and questions whose relevance changes from day to day.

The phased plan established channels and the first campus content before adding activities and services. A notice and its service entry had to work together: the post explained the offering, while the mini program carried the task. This planning made a limitation of group chat obvious to me. Messages move on; basic instructions still need a place people can find again.

Keep the service useful when partners want exposure

Students and partners come with different needs. Students want useful services, offers, inspiration and people to talk to; providers, suppliers and advertisers want access to the campus. I separated those groups in the planning so that “more exposure” could not quietly become the purpose of every interface area.

Location and timing became part of that distinction. A relevant offer could sit near a service choice, while a promotion repeated before every task would take space from the task itself. This changed how I read an operating plan: registration and group size described reach, but did not tell me whether a printing or delivery request was resolved.

The pilot screens, responsibility map and channel plan were the useful outputs to read together. They traced an entry to a service and then to support. I came away with a more concrete understanding of service design: changing the menu also changes what an announcement must explain and what a support response needs to know.

Tools, in context

WeChat Mini Programs
Campus service entries, location selection, and order navigation in the pilot interfaces.
WeChat Official Accounts & Groups
Service explanations, campus announcements, content publishing, and local support channels.
