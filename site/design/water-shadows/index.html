2023 · Virtual environment · Projection installation

水陌·渔影

A digital art project connecting a virtual landscape inspired by Hongfan Village, Qiandao Lake, with a fish-shaped projection installation.

以千岛湖洪畈村为背景，通过虚拟场景、粒子影像和鱼骨状投影装置，探索自然景观与数字空间的连接。

Year
2023
Practice
Virtual environment · Projection installation
My role
Outdoor scene and technical art; installation design and fabrication; video editing and project writing.

Outdoor scene and shared sky

Our plan was to develop several scenes around Hongfan Village by Qiandao Lake, combine them into a virtual environment, and render a video. I built the outdoor scene. Hanyi developed the interior exhibition space, and Wenkang modeled the architectural redevelopment. The sky and particle effects from my scene were shared across the project.

The outdoor composition used lake water, islands, brick walls, and dense vegetation. I wanted the vegetation to cover the remains of habitation and give the village a sense of abandonment. For the sky, I modified Ultra Dynamic Sky, overrode the blueprint function that retrieves the stars, replaced the skybox with imagery generated in Stable Diffusion, and adjusted its parameters to fit the scene.

Niagara fish and scene-depth points

The fish effect was my first use of Niagara scratch-pad scripting. I iterated on gathering, separating, blinking, and spline-following behaviors. Per-fish decisions allowed an instance to follow the group or leave it, so the flock did not move as a single fixed formation. The spline supplied a route while the individual behaviors introduced variation within it.

The point-cloud landscape used a separate input. A scene-capture component stored depth in the image alpha channel, and Niagara used the captured data to produce points. This effect represented the village geometry, whereas the fish system updated independent moving instances. The separate paths allow flock behavior to change without altering the depth representation of the environment.

Frame fabrication and material changes

We had discussed the installation form without assigning the fabrication work. Alongside the outdoor scene, I took on the Rhino structure using Hanyi and Wenkang’s ideas, worked through the geometry, and prepared cutting drawings. The first ribs were intended to use steel tubing. We bought tubing and a tube bender, but the bends were too rigid for the fish shape.

I proposed hard aluminum wire inside flexible metal tubing. The wire retained the curve and the tubing provided the outer surface. For the skin, hammered sheet metal required too much fabrication, while 3D printing was too expensive. We changed to projection mesh. This left the frame responsible for the outline and allowed the projected image to supply surface detail.

Changing the skin also changed the division of work. The frame only had to establish the fish’s outline; it no longer had to reproduce a detailed metal surface. The mesh provided a receiving surface for the image. I could then develop the changing texture in TouchDesigner while keeping the physical form fixed. Material choice and visual content had to be considered together, because the new surface made the projected image part of the object itself.

TouchDesigner flow-field projection

I built the projection effect in TouchDesigner. Geometry COMP instancing created the visible particles. Remap TOP looked up the flow-field force at the particle positions; that force was used to calculate acceleration, which was added to the current velocity. Updating the velocity made the particles follow the field. The field controlled motion, while instancing handled the repeated geometry.

The resulting particle image was projected onto the fish-shaped mesh. The mesh replaced a solid skin, so the installation combined the physical rib structure with a surface that changed over time. The projection photograph and network view show the two parts of this setup: the particle update in software and the image on the assembled frame.

I treat these as two distinct jobs when explaining the effect. Sampling the field determines how an instance moves; Geometry COMP determines how the instances become visible. The scene-depth point cloud in Unreal follows another route: it reads captured depth rather than this force field. Keeping those inputs separate is useful when tracing a visual problem back to its source—motion belongs to the field update, while the village’s point distribution belongs to scene capture.

What I would plan earlier

The last stage of fabrication took the three of us two late nights in the photography studio. The team had to finish the frame, fit the skin, and complete the projection while scene development, video editing, and writing were still part of the same workload.

I also wrote the course paper on immersive digital tourism around Hongfan Village from our group discussions and submitted it for the Challenge Cup.

For a similar project, I would estimate each person’s workload earlier and include assembly, projection adjustment, and finishing in the schedule. These tasks depend on each other: a completed scene does not finish the physical build, and a finished frame still needs its mesh fitted and its projection adjusted. I would first build one rib, fit the actual mesh, and test the image on it. That small trial would make the remaining work easier to estimate and give us a better basis for deciding which ideas to keep.
