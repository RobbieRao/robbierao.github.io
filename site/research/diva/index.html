RESEARCH / 2025

Between Real and Imagined: Developing a dynamic immersive virtual auditing framework for high-density urban health trails

DIVA structures the evaluation of urban health trails as an end-to-end experimental workflow: reconstruct a place, vary its design, observe an immersive walk and translate multimodal measurements into design feedback.

Unity · VR prototype development

Yuhao He, Tian Tian Lo, Fenggui Rao, Yuhao Li, and Hanzhe Bao.

eCAADe 2025

Paper↗Notebook↗

Photogrammetry for editable VR scenes

The reconstruction uses recordings from human-eye and bird’s-eye viewpoints of a Hong Kong health trail, combined in Polycam. Photogrammetry was selected after comparison with neural rendering because the reconstructed geometry needed to integrate with conventional scene models and support later analytical edits.

This choice affects my Unity/VR work directly. Greenery, facilities, and spatial sequence become experimental variables, so a scene needs to accept changes to those elements while preserving its surrounding structure. Geometric assets fit that editing workflow. Integration difficulty and restricted views in the neural-rendering alternatives considered made them less suitable for this use.

The representation choice also sets the scope of verification. Visual similarity can be checked against the captured trail, while editability concerns whether a designated intervention can be made without unintentionally changing other scene cues. Both matter when the same environment is reused across experimental conditions.

Factorial conditions and exposure sequence

DIVA proposes a 2 × 2 × 2 design: high or low greenery density, many or few facilities, and varying or unchanging spatial sequence. These factors produce eight combinations. Binary levels simplify the initial comparison and allow main effects and interactions to be specified, but they also omit intermediate degrees of spatial change.

The illustrative protocol assigns three randomly selected scenarios to a participant. Navigation pace is controlled, EDA and PPG are recorded continuously, and PRS-11 follows each exposure. A scenario identifier therefore needs to resolve to a factor combination, while the session record needs the order and exposure interval. The scene file alone cannot supply all of that information.

For the Unity client, the factors define permitted differences between conditions. Route, viewpoint, timing, and presentation should be checked against the intended comparison when a scene is changed. Extra visual detail has a cost here if it introduces a cue unrelated to the factor under study.

The matrix makes the comparisons explicit. Moving vertically changes greenery while retaining the facility level and spatial-sequence condition. Moving horizontally within a panel changes facilities. Moving between corresponding cells in the two panels changes spatial sequence. Density and arrangement must remain distinguishable; otherwise a contrast attributed to sequence could also contain a change in greenery or facilities.

The three assigned scenarios are a subset of the eight conditions. A participant’s session order therefore cannot be inferred from the condition number. For the VR record, the factor combination identifies the environment, while the exposure order identifies when that participant encountered it. Keeping both prevents a scene label from being used as a substitute for a session timeline.

Synchronizing physiological and subjective records

The proposed preprocessing uses NeuroKit2 for EDA filtering, artifact correction, and feature extraction. EDA is decomposed into tonic skin conductance level and phasic responses. Wrist-worn PPG is filtered and processed for pulse peaks and inter-beat intervals, from which measures such as SDNN and RMSSD are derived. These operations belong to the team’s analysis plan.

PRS-11 is completed after each virtual exposure using a five-point scale. It covers fascination, being-away, coherence, and scope. The questionnaire and continuous signals use different time units: one response set refers to an exposure, while signal features are computed from intervals within it. Both need the active condition and a recoverable exposure timeline.

That timing requirement reaches the VR implementation. Entering a scene, beginning the exposure, ending it, and displaying the questionnaire are distinct events. Synchronization and calibration remain deployment constraints in the framework; adding sensors without resolving these boundaries would leave the later analysis uncertain about which condition produced a response.

Scene loading and exposure are not the same interval. Loading, calibration, and the questionnaire can introduce responses unrelated to the target environment. The analysis needs the interval assigned to the controlled exposure, together with its condition, before extracting signal features or pairing them with PRS-11. This defines a recording boundary for the Unity workflow without depending on a particular sensor sampling rate.

Analysis plan and experimental constraints

The framework specifies ANOVA for main and interaction effects, random-forest regression for predictive importance, and K-means for response patterns across participants. These analyses answer different questions about the same conditions. DIVA remains a proposed protocol; the intervention trial is a later validation step.

VR increases control over ambient noise, pedestrian traffic, and weather compared with an outdoor test. That control also removes environmental cues present on the actual trail. The proposed comparison therefore trades some ecological detail for more explicit manipulation of spatial variables. Sensor calibration, synchronization, and fatigue from repeated exposure add practical limits.

A complete session is the useful development unit: scene entry, controlled navigation, exposure termination, questionnaire, and transition to the next assigned condition. My priority in the VR prototype is keeping that sequence consistent across the factorial combinations. Scene fidelity is useful when the environment remains editable and the comparison can still be reconstructed from the session record.
