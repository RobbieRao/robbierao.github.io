DisFace3DNet

Explainable Facial Attractiveness Prediction via 3D Component Disentanglement

Seven component routes connect a face photograph to an overall prediction. Their weighted contributions add up to the model’s score, making its use of visual evidence inspectable.

Fenggui Rao, Yan Luximon*, and Jie Zhang*.

arXiv:2610.11656 · Preprint

My workComponent-based model design and evaluation

Paper↗Code (soon)↗Notebook↗

What contributes to a prediction?

An overall rating does not show how neutral facial shape, skin appearance, hair, lighting, background, expression and pose contribute. DisFace3DNet gives those components designated inputs and numerical readouts, then makes them participate directly in the final prediction.

From separated inputs to an additive score

  1. Prepare seven representations

    3D reconstruction separates neutral geometry, albedo and illumination. Segmentation isolates hair and background; expression is represented as displacement from the same identity’s neutral mesh. Pose stays outside the neutral geometry as pitch, yaw and roll.

  2. Train component-specific routes

    EfficientNet-B0 encodes UV maps and the hair image; MLPs handle compact background and pose vectors. Five static scores are nonnegative, while expression and pose provide signed adjustments. Overall human ratings train the routes jointly, with weak semantic guidance for score direction.

  3. Freeze, calibrate, account

    After checkpoint selection, the encoders are frozen. Training-set statistics standardize the static and dynamic totals; constrained fusion fits two nonnegative weights and an intercept. Expanding that expression gives seven contributions that exactly reconstruct the unclipped prediction.

The preprint reports PCC 0.8904 ± 0.0063 across five folds, compared with mean human ratings. Matched controls examine representation and training choices; human comparisons examine whether component-score directions are understandable. Identity, skin and hair receive clearer directional support than expression. The contribution is explicit model accounting, not a claim that the learned components are independent human judgments or causal explanations.
